## Overview

What is a *liminal space*? Why do corridors, waiting rooms, vacant offices, and other transitional environments feel both familiar and unsettling? Can their atmospheres become deliberate subjects of architectural design?

**Liminal Anatomy: Decoding the Shared Perception of Indeterminacy** traces liminality from an anthropological concept through architectural environments to contemporary digital aesthetics. Through psychological and spatial analyses, it develops a framework for studying ambiguous environments, a design vocabulary for shaping them, and an interactive installation to test how people perceive them.

This repository documents a **2026 MArch (Post-Professional) thesis project** at the **John H. Daniels Faculty of Architecture, Landscape, and Design, University of Toronto**. It is a research-and-design portfolio, **not** an AI application or software implementation.

## Three research questions

1. **What is liminal space?** — Origins, architectural manifestations, and digital aesthetic culture.
2. **What does liminal space tell us?** — Psychological mechanisms and spatial features, explored through mapping and an exploratory perceptual survey.
3. **How can we apply liminal anatomy?** — Translating analysis into a *Kit of Parts* and the *Liminal Box* interactive exhibition.

## Key contributions

### TSEP Model — Time · Space · Event · Perception

An analytical framework for investigating the conditions and perceptions of liminal environments.

- **Time:** transitional moments, waiting, suspension.
- **Space:** thresholds, interstitial voids, circulation and non-places.
- **Event:** actions and occurrences that alter spatial meaning.
- **Perception:** emotional and psychological responses, such as uncanniness, nostalgia and alienation.

### Kit of Parts

A vocabulary of spatial, material, and technological design operations for composing and modifying liminal atmospheres.

### The Liminal Box

An interactive, collective perceptual experiment in which participants transform miniature spatial settings. Two study scenarios documented in the thesis are **The Office** and **The Playroom**.

## Research methods

Conceptual and precedent research · Psychological and spatial mapping · Exploratory survey at Daniels (April 2026) · TSEP analysis · Kit of Parts · Interactive installation.

**Methodological note:** The survey involved a primarily architecture-related group and does not constitute a statistically representative sample. The TSEP Model is an architectural research tool, not a clinically validated psychological assessment.
